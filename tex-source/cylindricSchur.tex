The family of (skew) cylindric Schur functions was introduced by
\name{Alexander Postnikov} in \cite{Postnikov2005}.
This is motivated by the fact that the coefficients in the Schur expansion
of such cylindric Schur functions are given by 3-point
\hyperref[gromovWittenInvariant]{Gromov--Witten invariants} of the
\hyperref[grassmannian]{Grassmannian}.
See also \cite{McNamara2006} for an introduction to this family of symmetric
functions.
\name{Ron M. Adin}, \name{Victor Reiner}, and \name{Yuval Roichman} study
cyclic descents for tableaux, including the cylindric descent framework
\cite{AdinReinerRoichman2017}.

The cylindric Schur functions are a subset of
\hyperref[schurAffineSkew]{skew affine Schur functions}, which was proved by
\name{Thomas Lam} in \cite[Prop.~33]{Lam2006}.


\subsection[cylindricSchurDefinition]{Definition}

A \defin{cylindric shape}\label{cylindricShape} $\lambda$ is an infinite
lattice path in $\setZ^2$, invariant under shifts by $(n-m,m)$, where
$m \in [1,n-1]$.
Let $C^{n,m}$ be the set of such paths.
Suppose $\lambda,\mu \in C^{n,m}$ and $\mu$ always lies weakly to the left of
$\lambda$.
Then we say that $\mu \subseteq \lambda$ and that $\lambda / \mu$ is a
\defin{cylindric skew shape}\label{cylindricSkewShape}.


A \defin{cylindric semistandard tableau}\label{cylindricSemistandardTableau}
of shape $\lambda / \mu$ is a sequence
\[
\mu = \lambda^0 \subseteq \lambda^1 \subseteq \dotsb \subseteq \lambda^{\ell} = \lambda
\]
such that each shape $\lambda^i/\lambda^{i-1}$ contains at most one box in each column.
Furthermore, we can think of placing the value $i$ in the boxes determined by
$\lambda^i/\lambda^{i-1}$.
The weight $(a_1,a_2,\dotsc,a_{\ell})$ of the tableau
is given by letting $a_i$ be the number of boxes in any $n-m$
consecutive columns of $\lambda^i/\lambda^{i-1}$.


The \defin{cylindric Schur function}\label{cylindricSchurFunction}
$\schurCylindric_{\lambda/\mu}(\xvec)$ is then defined as
\[
\schurCylindric_{\lambda/\mu}(\xvec)
= \sum_{T \in \mathrm{CSSYT}(\lambda/\mu)} \xvec^T
\]
where the sum is over all cylindric semistandard tableaux of shape
$\lambda/\mu$.
One can show via a variant of the Bender--Knuth involution
that all $\schurCylindric_{\lambda/\mu}(\xvec)$ are symmetric functions.

The chain $\lambda^0 \subseteq \dotsb \subseteq \lambda^{\ell}$ is a
periodic Gelfand--Tsetlin pattern, so cylindric tableaux of a fixed weight are
the lattice points of a
\hyperref[cylindricGTPatterns]{cylindric Gelfand--Tsetlin polytope}.
\name{Per Alexandersson} and \name{Ezgi Kantarcı Oğuz} use this to prove
saturation and polynomiality of stretched cylindric Kostka coefficients
\cite[Thm.~30 and Cor.~41]{AlexanderssonOguz2023x}; see
\hyperref[cylindricKostkaSaturation]{cylindric saturation}.

This implies invariance when shifting $3$ steps up and $4$ steps to the right.
We show two cylindric semistandard tableaux, of two different shapes,
where exactly one representative of each orbit of boxes under the shift action
has been assigned an integer.
Note that the second example shows that the \hyperref[schurSkew]{skew Schur
functions} are a proper subset of cylindric Schur functions.

\end{ytableau}

The first tableau has weight $(3,3,2,1,1)$ and the second one $(2,3,1,1)$.
The Schur expansion of the first shape gives
\[
\schurCylindric_{\lambda/\mu} = \schurS_{433} +
\schurS_{4321} -
\schurS_{33211} +
\schurS_{2221111} -
\schurS_{211111111}.
\]
\end{example*}

\name{Alexander Postnikov} uses the notation $\lambda/d/\mu$ to describe a
cylindric shape, where $\lambda$ and $\mu$ are partitions and $d\geq 0$
\cite{Postnikov2005}.
The cylindric shape is constructed as follows:
Draw the outline of the partition $\lambda$ in the plane, shifted $d$ steps
right and $d$ steps down, then draw the outline of $\mu$ without shift, and
extend both outlines periodically.

In the example above, the first shape can be described as $(6,3,3)/1/(6,3,1)$
and the second shape is just $(4,4,4)/0/(3,2,0)$, so that when $d=0$
the shape is a regular skew shape.

\name{Peter McNamara}'s running example \cite[p.~12]{McNamara2006}
illustrates another way to read off this notation.  For a cylindric shape on the
cylinder with $k=3$ and $n-k=4$, one first chooses a fundamental strip of
representatives.  In that strip the lower boundary determines
$\mu=(2,1)$, while the finite skew shape has outer partition
$\Lambda=(4,4,4,4,2,1,1)$.  Removing the top $7$-ribbon twice gives
$\Lambda[-1]=(4,4,4,1)$ and then $\Lambda[-2]=(3,3)$.  Hence this
cylindric shape is described as $(3,3)/2/(2,1)$.

For the poset structure on cylindric diagrams and its connection
to \hyperref[rootSystem]{root systems} and the \hyperref[bruhatOrder]{Bruhat order},
see \cite{NakadaSuzukiToyosawa2023x}.


Every cylindric Schur polynomial can be obtained as a
\hyperref[schurAffineSkew]{skew affine Schur function}.
This implies, in particular, that the support of the monomials in a skew Schur
function forms an \hyperref[MConvexity]{$M$-convex set}.

Note that cylindric Schur polynomials are not in general special cases of
\hyperref[pPartitionDefinition]{$(P,w)$-partitions}; see
\cite{McNamara2006}.  This is easily seen from the fact that all
$(P,w)$-partitions are positive in the fundamental quasisymmetric basis, while
\hyperref[cylindricSchurPositivity]{\enquote{most} cylindric Schur functions
are not}.

\name{Isaiah Siegl} defines cylindric $P$-tableaux for $(3+1)$-free posets
and corresponding $P$-analogues of cylindric Schur functions
\cite{Siegl2025}.  The resulting determinantal functions are weight
generating functions for cylindric $P$-tableaux and imply positivity for
certain sums of elementary-expansion coefficients of chromatic symmetric
functions of incomparability graphs.


\section[cylindricMurnaghanNakayama]{Cylindric Murnaghan--Nakayama rule}

\name{Per Alexandersson} and \name{Ezgi Kantarcı Oğuz} extend the
\hyperref[murnaghanNakayamaRule]{Murnaghan--Nakayama rule} to cylindric Schur
functions \cite{AlexanderssonOguz2023x}.  Since cylindric Schur functions are
in general not \hyperref[pPartitionDefinition]{$(P,w)$-partition} generating
functions, the proof instead uses inclusion--exclusion over the strict edges
of the poset of the shape, together with the
\hyperref[quasisymmetricPowerSumPsi]{quasisymmetric power-sum} expansion of
\name{Per Alexandersson} and \name{Robin Sulzgruber}
\cite[Thm.~6.2]{AlexanderssonSulzgruber2019}.  A related formula
appears in \cite[Eq.~(145)]{KorffPalazzo2020}.

As on the \hyperref[cylindricGTPatterns]{Gelfand--Tsetlin page}, we describe a
cylindric skew shape $D$ by $m$ consecutive rows forming a skew shape
$\lambda/\mu$, where row $j+m$ is row $j$ shifted $\ell$ columns to the left.
Put $N \coloneqq \ell+m$.
For $k \leq N$, a \defin{cylindric $k$-ribbon}\label{cylindricRibbon} is a
connected cylindric shape with $k$ boxes and no $2\times 2$ block of boxes; it
is a \defin{loop ribbon}\label{cylindricLoopRibbon} if $k=N$, so that it
wraps once around the cylinder.  The height of a non-loop ribbon is the number
of rows it meets minus one, and the height of a loop ribbon is $m$.

Peel loop ribbons off $D$ from the inner boundary: if the boxes of $D$ along
its inner boundary form a loop ribbon $L_1$ and $D\setminus L_1$ is nonempty,
remove $L_1$ and repeat.  This writes
$D = L_1 \cup \dotsb \cup L_s \cup F$, where we stop when $F$ is a loop ribbon
or the boxes along the inner boundary of $F$ do not form a loop ribbon.
Then $D$ is a \defin{stacked ribbon}\label{cylindricStackedRibbon} if $F$ is a
ribbon; it is \emph{pure} if $F$ is a loop ribbon and \emph{non-pure}
otherwise.  The height and width of a stacked ribbon are
\[
 \mathrm{ht}(D) \coloneqq (m+1)s + \mathrm{ht}(F),
 \qquad
 \mathrm{wd}(D) \coloneqq
 \begin{cases}
  \text{number of columns of the cylinder occupied by } D & \text{if $D$ is pure,}\\
  1 & \text{if $D$ is non-pure.}
 \end{cases}
\]
A \defin{cylindric ribbon tableau}\label{cylindricRibbonTableau} of shape $D$
and content $\nu=(\nu_1,\dotsc,\nu_r)$ is a filling of $D$ in which, for each
$j$, the boxes with entries at most $j$ form a cylindric shape with the same
inner boundary as $D$, and the boxes with entry $j$ form a stacked ribbon
$f_j$ with $\nu_j$ boxes.  Let $\mathrm{RT}(D,\nu)$ be the set of these
tableaux.

\begin{theorem}[Alexandersson--Kantarcı Oğuz {\cite[Thm.~16]{AlexanderssonOguz2023x}}]
\label{cylindricMurnaghanNakayamaRule}
For every cylindric skew shape $D$,
\[
\schurCylindric_D(\xvec) = \sum_{\nu} \frac{\powerSum_\nu(\xvec)}{z_\nu}
\sum_{f \in \mathrm{RT}(D,\nu)} \prod_{j=1}^{\length(\nu)}
(-1)^{\mathrm{ht}(f_j)} \, \mathrm{wd}(f_j),
\]
where $\nu$ ranges over partitions.
\end{theorem}

When $D$ is an ordinary skew shape, no loop ribbons occur and the theorem is
the classical Murnaghan--Nakayama rule for skew Schur functions.

For ordinary shapes, all tilings of $\lambda$ by $k$-ribbons have the same
total height parity, so the coefficient $\chi^{\lambda}_{(k^r)}$ is a sum of
terms of one sign \cite[Cor.~9]{White1983}, \cite[Thm.~2.7.27]{JamesKerber1984}.
On the cylinder this fails: there is a cylindric shape with six boxes and
exactly two tilings by $2$-ribbons, of total heights one and two, so the
coefficient of $\powerSum_{222}(\xvec)$ vanishes
\cite[Ex.~18]{AlexanderssonOguz2023x}.  Cancellation-freeness does hold for
a staircase inner boundary.
A cylindric shape is \defin{inner-strict}\label{cylindricInnerStrict} if its
inner boundary is a staircase, that is, its inner boundary alternates between
single down and right steps; then $\ell=m$ and $\mu_j-\mu_{j+1}=1$ for all $j$.

\begin{theorem}[Alexandersson--Kantarcı Oğuz {\cite[Cor.~24]{AlexanderssonOguz2023x}}]
\label{cylindricMNCancellationFree}
Let $D$ be an inner-strict cylindric skew shape and let $k\leq N$.
If $D$ can be tiled by cylindric $k$-ribbons, then all such tilings have the
same total height parity.  In particular, the coefficient of
$\powerSum_{(k^r)}(\xvec)/z_{(k^r)}$ in $\schurCylindric_D(\xvec)$ is a sum of
terms of the same sign.
\end{theorem}

\section[schurToric]{Toric Schur polynomials}

\begin{polydata}{schurToric}

  Rating & 5 \\

A shape $\lambda/\mu \in C^{n,m}$ is called \defin{toric}\label{toricShape} if
every column contains at most $m$ boxes.
Cylindric Schur functions indexed by such shapes
are referred to as \defin{toric Schur polynomials}\label{toricSchurPolynomial}.
All the usual skew shapes are toric.

The specialization
\[
\schurCylindric_{\lambda/d/\mu}(\xvec)(x_1,\dotsc,x_m) \coloneqq
\schurCylindric_{\lambda/d/\mu}(x_1,\dotsc,x_m,0,\dotsc)
\]
is nonzero if and only if the shape $\lambda/d/\mu$ is toric.
These polynomials, in a finite set of variables,
are referred to as \hyperref[toricSchurPolynomial]{toric Schur polynomials}.



\subsection[toricSchurIntoSchur]{Schur expansion}

Postnikov proves that when $\lambda/d/\mu$ is a toric shape in $C^{n,m}$
the coefficients $C_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda,d}$ in the Schur expansion
\[
\schurCylindric_{\lambda/d/\mu}(x_1,\dotsc,x_m) =
\sum_\nu C_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda,d} \schurS_\nu(x_1,\dotsc,x_m)
\]
are non-negative integers given by certain
\defin{Gromov--Witten invariants}\label{gromovWittenInvariant}.


\begin{problem}[Postnikov, \cite{Postnikov2005}]
It is an open problem to find a (positive) combinatorial rule for $C_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda,d}$.
Note that such a rule would generalize the classical
\hyperref[littlewoodRichardsonCoefficient]{Littlewood--Richardson rule}.
\end{problem}

Postnikov also introduces a certain family of
\defin{toric Specht modules}\label{toricSpechtModule} and conjectures that
they decompose into irreducible \hyperref[spechtModules]{Specht modules} with
multiplicities given by the $C_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda,d}$.


The coefficients $C_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda,d}$ can be computed as an
\hyperref[littlewoodRichardsonFromKostant]{alternating sum of Kostka coefficients},
see \cite[Cor.~1.4]{Korff2009x}.

Another formula uses an alternating sum of regular
\hyperref[littlewoodRichardsonCoefficient]{Littlewood--Richardson
coefficients}; see
\cite[Eq.~(21)]{BertramCiocanFontanineFulton1999}.
They show that for $\lambda, \mu,\nu \subseteq \ell \times k$,
$|\lambda|+|\mu| = |\nu|+mn$,
\[
C_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu,m}
= \sum_{\rho} \sign(\rho/\nu) c^{\rho}_{\lambda,\mu}, \qquad
\sign(\rho/\nu) \coloneqq \prod_{j} (-1)^{k-\mathrm{width}(r_j)},
\]
where the sum is over all diagrams $\rho$ obtained from
$\nu$ by adding $m$ ribbons, each ribbon $r_j$ containing $n$ boxes and
starting from the first row.


The $C_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda,d}$ are closely related to
\defin{fusion coefficients}\label{fusionCoefficient}, as seen in
\cite{Korff2009x}.
For some positivity results on fusion coefficients, see \cite{MorseSchilling2012}.


Another related notion is the \hyperref[quantumKostka]{quantum Kostka coefficients}.


\subsection[cylindricSchurPositivity]{Cylindric Schur positivity}

\name{Peter McNamara} \cite{McNamara2006} asked for which cylindric shapes
$\schurCylindric_{\lambda/d/\mu}(\xvec)$ are
\hyperref[schurPositivity]{Schur-positive} as proper symmetric functions in an
infinite alphabet, and conjectured that
each cylindric skew Schur function can be positively expanded in
certain \emph{non-skew} cylindric Schur functions, with coefficients being
\hyperref[gromovWittenInvariant]{Gromov--Witten invariants}.
\name{Peter McNamara} proves that only ordinary skew Schur functions are
Schur-positive.
In fact, these are the only cylindric Schur functions that are positive in
the \hyperref[gessel]{Gessel fundamental quasisymmetric basis}; see
\cite[Thm.~5.7]{McNamara2006}.


McNamara's conjecture was answered in \cite{Lee2017} by the following theorem:
\begin{equation*}
\schurCylindric_{\lambda/d/\mu}(\xvec) = \sum_{\nu \in C^{n,m}, e\geq 0}
c^{\lambda/d/\mu}_{\nu/e/\emptyset} \schurCylindric_{\nu/e/\emptyset}(\xvec).
\end{equation*}
The coefficients are all non-negative and satisfy
$c^{\lambda/d/\mu}_{\nu/e/\emptyset}
= c^{\lambda/d-1/\mu}_{\nu/e-1/\emptyset}$ whenever $e \gt 0$.
Furthermore, $c^{\lambda/d/\mu}_{\nu/0/\emptyset} =
C_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda,d}$.

For a short proof, see \cite{Dobner2026x}, where the structure coefficients
are interpreted using \hyperref[fusionCoefficient]{fusion coefficients}.

\section[cylindricSchurSeeAlso]{See also}

\name{Shane Chern}, \name{Trieu Nguyen}, \name{Chanh Tran}, and
\name{Tanay Wakhare} study the rank-three cylindric-partition series
\[
 \sum_{\Lambda\text{ of profile }c}z^{\max(\Lambda)}q^{|\Lambda|},
\]
where $|\Lambda|$ is the total size and $\max(\Lambda)$ the largest part.
They obtain sum expressions using a rational analogue of an
$A_2^{(1)}$ Rogers--Ramanujan identity, proving the specified conjectures of
Kanade--Russell and Warnaar
\cite[Thm.~1 and Cors.~2--3]{ChernNguyenTranWakhare2026x}.

\name{Christian Korff} discusses a connection with a \hyperref[6vertexModel]{6-vertex model}
and Hecke characters, see \cite{Korff2020}.
