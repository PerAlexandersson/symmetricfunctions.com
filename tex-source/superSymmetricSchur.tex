The \defin{hook Schur functions}\label{hookSchurFunction}, also known as
\emph{supersymmetric Schur functions}, are characters of the Lie superalgebra
$\mathrm{gl}(m/n)$; see \cite{Kac1977}.
The hook Schur functions were introduced by \name{Allan Berele} and
\name{Amitai Regev} in 1983; see \cite{BereleRegev1983}.
A good overview of this area can be found in the PhD thesis by
\name[E. M. Moens]{Els M. Moens} \cite{Moens2007}.

This is also the sixth variant of Schur functions considered in
\cite[Eq.~6.19]{Macdonald1992}.
Both alphabets here consist of commuting variables. This differs from
\hyperref[schurSuperspace]{Schur polynomials in superspace},
which involve anticommuting variables.

\name{Slava Naprienko} introduces free fermionic Schur functions
$\schurS_{\lambda/\mu;\avec,\bvec}(\xvec/\yvec)$
\cite{Naprienko2024}.  These depend on two alphabets and two sequences of
parameters, and specialize to several familiar families, including factorial,
supersymmetric, and dual Schur functions.  The construction is based on a
free-fermionic six-vertex model and gives supersymmetric Cauchy identities,
determinantal formulas, and flagged formulas.
\name{Shinsuke Iwao} gives a free-fermionic presentation of
\defin{multi-Schur functions}\label{multiSchurFunction} \cite{Iwao2023}.
This family generalizes
supersymmetric Schur functions, flagged Schur functions, and refined dual
Grothendieck functions, and Iwao gives a method for expanding multi-Schur
functions in refined dual Grothendieck polynomials.
\name{Helen Riedtmann} proves overlap identities for Littlewood--Schur
functions \cite{Riedtmann2018x}.  These are closely related to the
supersymmetric, or hook, Schur functions; the identities are obtained from
the Moens--Van der Jeugt determinantal formula by Laplace expansion.

\subsection[superSymmetricFunctions]{Supersymmetric functions}

We follow the definitions in \cite{MoensJeugt2003}.  Let
$\xvec = (x_1,\dotsc,x_m)$ and $\yvec = (y_1,\dotsc,y_n)$.
A function $f(\xvec,\yvec)$ is
\defin{doubly symmetric}\label{doublySymmetricFunction}
if it is symmetric in each alphabet.
We let $\spaceSym(\xvec/\yvec)$ denote the subspace of doubly symmetric functions
with the property that substituting $x_1=t$, $y_1=-t$ results in an
expression independent of $t$.
We refer to functions in this subspace as the
\defin{supersymmetric functions}\label{supersymmetricFunction}.
For example, $x_1+x_2-y_1-y_2$ is doubly symmetric in $2+2$ variables,
while $x_1+x_2 + y_1 + y_2$ is also supersymmetric.


The \defin{complete homogeneous supersymmetric functions}
\label{completeHomogeneousSupersymmetricFunction} are defined as
\[
\completeH_r(\xvec/\yvec) \coloneqq
\sum_{j=0}^r \completeH_{j}(\xvec)\elementaryE_{r-j}(\yvec).
\]
Similarly, the \defin{elementary supersymmetric functions}
\label{elementarySupersymmetricFunction} are defined as
\[
\elementaryE_r(\xvec/\yvec) \coloneqq
\sum_{j=0}^r \elementaryE_j(\xvec)\completeH_{r-j}(\yvec).
\]
The \defin{supersymmetric power-sum polynomials}
\label{supersymmetricPowerSumPolynomial} are defined as
\[
\powerSum_r(\xvec/\yvec) \coloneqq
\powerSum_r(\xvec) + (-1)^{r-1} \powerSum_r(\yvec).
\]
The \defin{supersymmetric monomial polynomials}
\label{supersymmetricMonomialPolynomial} are defined as
\[
\monomial_\lambda(\xvec/\yvec) =
\sum_{\mu\cup \nu = \lambda}
\monomial_{\mu}(\xvec) \omega(\monomial_{\nu}(\yvec)).
\]
All these give bases for $\spaceSym(\xvec/\yvec)$.

\subsection[hookSchurTableauDef]{Tableau definition}

There is also a definition in terms of fillings of a Ferrers diagram of shape
$\lambda$.
We fill the shape with entries
\[
1 \lt 2 \lt \dotsb \lt k \lt 1' \lt 2' \lt \dotsb \lt l'.
\]
A filling in $SST(\lambda)$ is defined as a filling of $\lambda$ with entries
in the alphabet above such that rows and columns are weakly increasing.
Furthermore, the unprimed entries must be strictly increasing down columns,
and the primed entries must be strictly increasing along rows.
The weight $x^{w_x(T)} y^{w_y(T)}$ keeps track of the primed and
the unprimed alphabets. We have
\[
\schurHook_{\lambda/\mu}(\xvec/\yvec) \coloneqq
\sum_{T \in SST(\lambda/\mu)} x^{w_x(T)} y^{w_y(T)}
\]
where the expansion in the last sum is in terms of the
classical \hyperref[schurS]{Schur functions}.

\subsection[schurHookJacobiTrudi]{Jacobi--Trudi identity}

A Jacobi--Trudi type formula for hook Schur functions was proved in
\cite{PragaczThorup1992}, and it also follows from
\cite[Thm.~4.5]{Kwon2008}.
The \defin{$(m,n)$-hook Schur functions}\label{mnHookSchurFunction}
are then given as
\[
\schurHook_{\lambda/\mu}(\xvec/\yvec) \coloneqq
\det[ \completeH_{\lambda_i-\mu_j + j - i}(\xvec/\yvec) ]_{
  1\leq i,j \leq \length(\lambda)}.
\]
There is also the dual version of this identity.

The functions $\schurHook_\lambda(\xvec/\yvec)$ are identically zero whenever
$\lambda_{m+1} \geq n$.

\name{Alibek Adilzhan} and \name{Damir Yeliussizov} give
Hamel--Goulden type ribbon-decomposition determinant formulas for flagged
supersymmetric Schur functions \cite{AdilzhanYeliussizov2025x}.
Their functions use two doubly infinite alphabets
$\yvec=(y_i)_{i\in\setZ}$ and $\zvec=(z_i)_{i\in\setZ}$.  Let $\lambda/\mu$
be a skew shape with $n=\lambda_1$ columns, and let
$\avec=(a_1,\dotsc,a_n)$ and $\bvec=(b_1,\dotsc,b_n)$ be integer
\defin{column flags}, which must satisfy mild compatibility conditions
\cite[Def.~3.1]{AdilzhanYeliussizov2025x}.  The
\defin{flagged supersymmetric Schur function}\label{flaggedSupersymmetricSchur}
$\mathsf{S}^{\avec,\bvec}_{\lambda/\mu}(\yvec/\zvec)$ is defined by a dual
Jacobi--Trudi determinant in supersymmetric elementary functions of
consecutive variables \cite[Def.~3.2]{AdilzhanYeliussizov2025x}.  It has the
tableau formula
\[
  \mathsf{S}^{\avec,\bvec}_{\lambda/\mu}(\yvec/\zvec)
  =\sum_{T}\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda/\mu}\bigl(y_{T(i,j)}-z_{T(i,j)+j-i}\bigr),
\]
where $T$ runs over the semistandard tableaux of shape $\lambda/\mu$ with
integer entries satisfying $a_j\leq T(i,j)\leq b_j$ in column $j$.  This is
\cite[Thm.~4.5]{AdilzhanYeliussizov2025x} after summing over the choice of
primed or unprimed letter in each box.  For $\zvec=0$ it is a
\hyperref[flaggedSchurPolynomialDefinition]{flagged Schur function}, and
without flag bounds it is a supersymmetric Schur function.

The determinant formulas use \emph{outer ribbon decompositions}
$(\theta_1,\dotsc,\theta_k)$ of $\lambda/\mu$: partitions of the diagram into
ribbons whose heads (bottom-left cells) lie on the left or bottom boundary and
whose tails (top-right cells) lie on the top or right boundary.  In such a
decomposition, all cells of a given content $c=j-i$ continue in the same
direction, up or right.  This defines the \defin{cutting strip}, the ribbon
with the same contents in which the cell of content $c$ goes in that direction.
For $p\leq q+1$, let $\Theta(p,q)$ be the part of the cutting strip with
contents in $[p,q]$, empty if $p=q+1$.  Then
$\theta_i\#\theta_j\coloneqq\Theta(c(\text{head of }\theta_i),c(\text{tail of }\theta_j))$,
and $\theta_i\#\theta_j$ is undefined if
$c(\text{head of }\theta_i)>c(\text{tail of }\theta_j)+1$.
Its boxes keep their contents from $\lambda/\mu$, and these contents are used
in the tableau formula for the matrix entries.  Cut $\theta_i\#\theta_j$ into
its columns from left to right.  For its $r$-th column, let $m$ be the leftmost
cell of $\lambda/\mu$ with the content of the top box of that column, and let
$M$ be the rightmost cell of $\lambda/\mu$ with the content of the bottom box.
The \defin{induced ribbon flags} are then
\[
  a^{ij}_r=a_{\mathrm{col}(m)}-\mu'_{\mathrm{col}(m)}-1+\mathrm{row}(m),
  \qquad
  b^{ij}_r=b_{\mathrm{col}(M)}-\lambda'_{\mathrm{col}(M)}+\mathrm{row}(M)
\]
\cite[Def.~5.4]{AdilzhanYeliussizov2025x}.

\begin{theorem}[Adilzhan--Yeliussizov {\cite[Thm.~5.8]{AdilzhanYeliussizov2025x}}]
For every outer ribbon decomposition $(\theta_1,\dotsc,\theta_k)$ of a
connected skew shape $\lambda/\mu$,
\[
  \mathsf{S}^{\avec,\bvec}_{\lambda/\mu}(\yvec/\zvec)
  =\det\left[\mathsf{S}^{\avec^{ij},\bvec^{ij}}_{\theta_i\#\theta_j}(\yvec/\zvec)\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq k},
\]
where an empty $\theta_i\#\theta_j$ contributes $1$ and an undefined one
contributes $0$.
\end{theorem}

Decomposing into rows or into hooks and strips gives Jacobi--Trudi-type and
skew Giambelli-type determinants
\cite[Cor.~7.1 and Cor.~7.3]{AdilzhanYeliussizov2025x}.  A specialization of
the variables turns these into determinantal formulas for dual refined
canonical stable Grothendieck polynomials
\cite[Prop.~3.5 and Cor.~5.11]{AdilzhanYeliussizov2025x}.

\begin{example*}[A flagged Giambelli determinant]
% Checked symbolically against the determinant definition (Def. 3.2).
Let $\lambda=(2,2)$ with column flags $\avec=(1,1)$ and $\bvec=(2,2)$.  The
contents and the outer decomposition into the hook $\theta_1$ and the single
box $\theta_2$ are
\begin{figure}
\begin{ytableau}
0 & 1 \\
-1 & 0
\end{ytableau}
%
\begin{ytableau}
1 & 1 \\
1 & *(lightgray) 2
\end{ytableau}
\end{figure}
Contents $-1$ go up while contents $0$ and $1$ go right, so $\theta_1\#\theta_1$
is the hook $(2,1)$ with contents $-1,0,1$, $\theta_1\#\theta_2$ is a vertical
domino with contents $-1,0$, $\theta_2\#\theta_1$ is a horizontal domino with
contents $0,1$, and $\theta_2\#\theta_2$ is one box of content $0$.  The
induced flags are
\[
  (\avec^{11},\bvec^{11})=((1,1),(2,1)),\quad
  (\avec^{12},\bvec^{12})=((1),(2)),\quad
  (\avec^{21},\bvec^{21})=((1,1),(2,1)),\quad
  (\avec^{22},\bvec^{22})=((1),(2)).
\]
The flag $b^{11}_2=1$ forces the box of content $1$ in the hook to contain
$1$.  The admissible tableaux for the four entries are
\begin{figure}
\begin{ytableau}
1 & 1 \\
2
\end{ytableau}
%
\begin{ytableau}
1 \\
2
\end{ytableau}
%
\begin{ytableau}
1 & 1
\end{ytableau}
%
\begin{ytableau}
1
\end{ytableau}
%
\begin{ytableau}
2
\end{ytableau}
\end{figure}
so the theorem gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mathsf{S}^{\avec,\bvec}_{(2,2)}(\yvec/\zvec)
  &=\det\begin{bmatrix}
    (y_1-z_1)(y_1-z_2)(y_2-z_1) & (y_1-z_1)(y_2-z_1)\\
    (y_1-z_1)(y_1-z_2) & y_1+y_2-z_1-z_2
  \end{bmatrix}\\
  &=(y_1-z_1)(y_1-z_2)(y_2-z_1)(y_2-z_2).
\end{aligned}
\]
This agrees with the tableau formula, since the only admissible tableau of
shape $(2,2)$ has rows $1\,1$ and $2\,2$.  Without flags and with $\zvec=0$,
the same decomposition gives the Giambelli identity
$\schurS_{22}=\schurS_{21}\schurS_{1}-\schurS_{11}\schurS_{2}$.
\end{example*}

\subsection[hookSchurPlethDef]{Plethysm definition}

The $(m,n)$-hook Schur functions can be
defined in \hyperref[plethysm]{plethystic notation} as
\[
\schurHook_\lambda(x_1,\dotsc,x_m/y_1,\dotsc,y_n) \coloneqq
\schurS_\lambda(X - t Y) \vert_{t=-1}
\]
where $X= x_1+\dotsb+x_m$ and $Y= y_1+\dotsb+y_n$, see
\cite[Eq.~(55)]{YangRemmel1998}.



\subsection[schurHookProperties]{Properties}

The following four properties uniquely characterize the hook Schur functions,
see \cite{Macdonald1995} and \cite{MoensJeugt2003}.

\begin{itemize}

\item
(Homogeneity)
The polynomial $\schurHook_{\lambda}(\xvec/\yvec)$ is homogeneous of degree
$|\lambda|$.

\item
(Factorization) If $\lambda_m\geq n \geq \lambda_{m+1}$
so that $\lambda = (n^m + \tau)\cup \eta$, then
\[
\schurHook_{\lambda}(\xvec/\yvec) =
\schurS_{\tau}(\xvec)\schurS_{\eta'}(\yvec)
\prod_{i=1}^m\prod_{j=1}^n (x_i+y_j).
\]

\item
(Cancellation) We have that
\[
\schurHook_{\lambda}(x_1,\dotsc,x_{m-1},t/y_1,\dotsc,y_{n-1},-t)
=\schurHook_{\lambda}(x_1,\dotsc,x_{m-1}/y_1,\dotsc,y_{n-1}).
\]

\item
(Restriction) We have that
\[
\schurHook_{\lambda}(x_1,\dotsc,x_{m-1},0/\yvec)
=\schurHook_{\lambda}(x_1,\dotsc,x_{m-1}/\yvec)
\]
and
\[
\schurHook_{\lambda}(\xvec/y_1,\dotsc,y_{n-1},0)
=\schurHook_{\lambda}(\xvec/y_1,\dotsc,y_{n-1}).
\]
\end{itemize}

We have the following properties of the hook Schur functions,
see e.g., \cite{YangRemmel1998}.

\begin{itemize}
\item
$\schurHook_{\lambda/\mu}(\xvec/\emptyset) = \schurS_{\lambda/\mu}(\xvec)$.

\item
$\schurHook_{\lambda/\mu}(\emptyset/\yvec) = \schurS_{\lambda'/\mu'}(\yvec)$.

\item
$\schurHook_{\lambda/\mu}(\xvec/\yvec) = \schurHook_{\lambda'/\mu'}(\yvec/\xvec)$.

\item
$\schurHook_{\lambda/\mu}(\xvec/\yvec) =
\sum_{\mu \subseteq \nu \subseteq \lambda}
\schurS_{\nu/\mu}(\xvec) \schurS_{\lambda'/\nu'}(\yvec)$.
\end{itemize}

\name{Nohra Hage} gives a super Littlewood--Richardson type rule for super
Schur functions over signed alphabets \cite{Hage2025}.  The coefficients are
interpreted using super Young tableaux.
\name{Dang Tuan Hiep} and \name{Khai-Hoan Nguyen-Dang} prove that
supersymmetric Schur polynomials have the saturated Newton polytope property
\cite{HiepNguyenDang2025x}.  Their proof encodes the tableau support by a
totally unimodular polyhedron and then applies the Hoffman--Kruskal
integrality criterion.

A Weyl-type formula for $\schurHook_{\lambda}(\xvec/\yvec)$ as a quotient
of determinants is given in \cite[Eq.~(1.17)]{MoensJeugt2003}.
This is referred to as the \defin{Sergeev--Pragacz formula}
\label{sergeevPragaczFormula},
proved by \name{Alexander Sergeev} and independently in \cite{JeugtHughesKingThierry1990}.
A skew version was proved later in 1995 by \name{Angèle Hamel} and \name{Ian Goulden}.

\name{Mikhail Khovanov} explains how an extension of the
Robert--Wagner foam evaluation to overlapping theta-foams recovers the
Sergeev--Pragacz formula for supersymmetric Schur functions
\cite{Khovanov2020x}.  The same framework also connects Schur and
supersymmetric Schur determinants with Hankel and Toeplitz matrices arising
from two-dimensional topological theories.

\hyperref[thomPolynomials]{Thom polynomials} give another geometric setting
where Schur functions in a difference of alphabets occur naturally: the Chern
classes are those of a virtual bundle, and Schur expansions of Thom
polynomials often have strong positivity properties.


In \cite[Thm.~4.4]{Remmel1987}, a version of the
hook-content formula is proved where an expression for
\[
\sum_{k,l\geq 0}
t^k s^l \schurHook_\lambda(1,q,q^2,\dotsc,q^k / 1,p,p^2,\dotsc,p^l)
\]
is given.




\subsection[schurHookCauchy]{Cauchy identity}

In \cite{BereleRemmel1985}, the following Cauchy-type identity is proved.
\[
\sum_{\lambda}
\schurHook_\lambda(\xvec / \svec)
\schurHook_\lambda(\yvec / \tvec) =
\prod_{i,j} \frac{1+x_i t_j}{1-x_iy_j} \frac{1+y_i s_j}{1-s_i t_j}
\]
A bijective proof can be found in \cite{YangRemmel1998}.


\subsection[schurHookLittlewoodFormula]{Littlewood formula}
\name{M. Yang} and \name{Jeffrey Remmel} \cite{YangRemmel1998} prove that
\[
\prod_{i\lt j} (1-x_i x_j)
\prod_{i\gt j} (1+y_i y_j)
\prod_{i, j} \frac{1}{1-x_iy_j}
= 1 +\sum_{\alpha} \schurHook_\alpha(x_1,\dotsc,x_m/y_1,\dotsc,y_n)
\]
where we sum over all partitions of the form
\[
\alpha =
\begin{pmatrix}
a_1  & a_2 & \dotsc & a_r \\
a_1+1& a_2+1& \dotsc & a_r+1
\end{pmatrix}
\]
in \hyperref[prelimPartitions]{Frobenius notation}.


\subsection[schurHookGeneralizations]{Generalizations}

\begin{polydata}{hookSchurQS}

A quasisymmetric refinement is introduced in \cite{MasonNiese2018},
and the symmetric hook Schur functions can be
decomposed into such quasisymmetric counterparts.
That is
\[
\schurHook_\lambda(x_1,\dotsc,x_m/y_1,\dotsc,y_n) =
\sum_{\alpha \sim \lambda} \schurHookQS_\alpha(x_1,\dotsc,x_m/y_1,\dotsc,y_n)
\]

The quasisymmetric hook Schur functions are positive in the
super Gessel fundamental basis, see \cite[Thm.~4.2]{MasonNiese2018}.
They conjecture that the structure constants for
quasisymmetric hook Schur functions are the same as for the
quasisymmetric Schur functions.

\name{Susanna Fishel}, \name{Jessica Gatica}, \name{Luc Lapointe}, and
\name[Maria Elena Pinto]{Mar{\'i}a Elena Pinto} study the fundamental
quasisymmetric functions in superspace \cite{FishelGaticaLapointePinto2025}.
They describe the coproduct, product, and antipode on this fundamental basis.
They also extend Gessel's expansion of Schur functions into fundamental
quasisymmetric functions to skew Schur functions in superspace.
\name{Susanna Fishel}, \name{Luc Lapointe}, and
\name[Maria Elena Pinto]{Mar{\'i}a Elena Pinto} establish Hopf-algebra
structures on symmetric and quasisymmetric functions in superspace
\cite{FishelLapointePinto2019}.  Here superspace means adjoining
anticommuting variables to the ordinary commuting variables.
\name{John Lentfer} proves the diagonal supersymmetry conjecture for
coinvariant rings \cite{Lentfer2025x}.  More generally, bosonic-fermionic
coinvariant rings for finite groups carry a
$U(\mathfrak{gl}(k|j))\otimes \setC[G]$-module structure, and their character
series expand in super Schur functions with universal coefficients.

\name{Dmitry Galakhov}, \name{Alexei Morozov}, and \name{Nikita Tselousov}
give a combinatorial construction of supersymmetric Schur, Jack, and
Macdonald polynomial families indexed by super-Young diagrams
\cite{GalakhovMorozovTselousov2024}.  Their super-Macdonald polynomials
recover the ordinary Macdonald polynomials in the purely even case and carry
a representation of a super-algebra analogue of the Ding--Iohara--Miki
algebra.

\bigskip

There is also a generalization in the direction of supersymmetric Schur functions
indexed by \emph{composite partitions}.  A conjectured Jacobi--Trudi formula
was presented in \cite{Moens2007} and later proved in \cite{BinhDungHai2018}.

In \cite{Shigechi2017x}, \name{Keiichi Shigechi} introduces the big Schur functions.
These are closely related to \hyperref[schurP]{Schur's P functions},
and the \hyperref[hookSchur]{supersymmetric Schur functions}.

We consider fillings of $\lambda$ with entries in
the alphabet $1' \lt 1 \lt 2' \lt 2 \lt \dotsb $
such that
\begin{itemize}
\item
	each row has at most one marked $i$ for every $i=1,2,\dotsc$;
\item
	each column has at most one unmarked $i$, for every $i=1,2,\dotsc$,
\item
	entries in rows and columns are weakly increasing.
\end{itemize}
We let $SSShYT(\lambda)$ denote the set of such fillings.

Then the \defin{big Schur function}\label{bigSchurFunction}
$\bigSchur_{\lambda}(\xvec)$
is defined as
\[
\bigSchur_{\lambda}(\xvec) = \sum_{T \in SSShYT(\lambda)} \xvec_T
\]
where the weight of a tableau is obtained by treating primed entries as unprimed.

We can also realize $\bigSchur_{\lambda}(\xvec)$ as the specialization $\schurHook_{\lambda}(\xvec/\xvec)$.


\begin{example}
We have
\[
\bigSchur_{211}
= 12 \monomial_{32}+4 \monomial_{41}+40 \monomial_{221}
+24 \monomial_{311}+80 \monomial_{2111}+160 \monomial_{11111}.
\]
\end{example}


Of course, there is a Jacobi--Trudi identity (also valid in the skew case):
\[
\bigSchur_{\lambda}(\xvec) = \det\left[ r_{\lambda_i -i +j}(\xvec) \right]_{1\leq i, j \leq \length(\lambda)}
\]
where
\[
r_k(\xvec) = \sum_{\mu \vdash k} 2^{\length(\mu)} \monomial_{\mu}(\xvec).
\]
Alternatively,
\[
\sum_{k \geq 0} t^k r_k(\xvec) =
\prod_{i} \frac{1+x_i t}{1-x_i t}.
\]

Shigechi also gives expansions of skew big Schur functions in products of
skew \hyperref[schurP]{Schur $P$-functions}.  Equivalently, after the
standard powers of $2$ relating $P$- and \hyperref[schurQ]{Schur
$Q$-functions}, these can be written in products of skew Schur
$Q$-functions.  The same work gives determinant and Pfaffian Giambelli
formulae for skew big Schur functions.



\section[schurSuperspace]{Schur polynomials in superspace}

We work with commuting variables $x_1,\dotsc,x_N$ and anticommuting
variables $\theta_1,\dotsc,\theta_N$:
$\theta_i\theta_j=-\theta_j\theta_i$ and $\theta_i^2=0$.
A symmetric superpolynomial is invariant under simultaneous permutations
of the pairs $(x_i,\theta_i)$.
Its indexing \defin{superpartition}
$\Lambda=(\Lambda^a;\Lambda^s)$ consists of a strictly decreasing
sequence of nonnegative integers $\Lambda^a$ and an ordinary partition
$\Lambda^s$; $\length(\Lambda^a)$ is the degree in the $\theta$-variables.

The \defin{Schur superpolynomials} are two limits of the
Macdonald polynomials in superspace:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 s_\Lambda(\xvec,\theta)&=P_\Lambda(\xvec,\theta;0,0),\\
 \bar s_\Lambda(\xvec,\theta)
 &=\lim_{t\to\infty}P_\Lambda(\xvec,\theta;t,t).
 \end{aligned}
\]
When $\Lambda^a$ is empty, both reduce to the ordinary
$\schurS_{\Lambda^s}(\xvec)$.
\name{Olivier Blondeau-Fournier} and \name{Pierre Mathieu}
proposed tableau descriptions and Pieri rules
\cite{BlondeauFournierMathieu2015}.
\name{Miles Jones} and \name{Luc Lapointe} subsequently proved
Pieri rules and derived tableau generating functions
\cite{JonesLapointe2017}; in particular, their Remark~21 identifies
the proof of the earlier Pieri conjecture.
These superspace families are distinct from the
\hyperref[hookSchur]{hook Schur functions} in two commuting alphabets
and are related to the \hyperref[macdonaldPMulti]{double Macdonald polynomials}
through a stable limit.

\section[factorialHookSchur]{Factorial supersymmetric Schur polynomials}

\begin{polydata}{factorialHookSchur}

  Space    & SuperSym \\
  Basis    & True \\
  Rating   & 1 \\
  Bib      & Molev1996 \\
  Year     & 1996 \\
  Keywords & jacobi-trudi,tableaux,skew \\
  Symbol   & $\schurS_\lambda(\xvec/\yvec|a)$ \\
  Category & Schur \\
\end{polydata}


In \cite[Def.~1.1]{Molev1996}, \name{Alexander Molev} introduces a factorial
version of supersymmetric Schur functions.
They can be described via tableaux and Jacobi--Trudi identities.
Molev also proves a characterization theorem and a Sergeev--Pragacz type formula,
and introduces the \defin{shifted supersymmetric Schur polynomials}
\label{shiftedSupersymmetricSchurPolynomial}.


In \cite{FoleyKing2020x}, the authors present determinant identities
for skew factorial supersymmetric Schur functions.
\name{Soichi Okada} introduces generalized Schur $P$- and $Q$-functions
associated with a polynomial sequence \cite{Okada2019x}.  These are
recorded as \hyperref[generalizedSchurP]{generalized Schur $P$- and
$Q$-functions}.  They are Macdonald's ninth variation for the $P$/$Q$
setting, and include ordinary Schur $P$/$Q$-functions, factorial
$P$/$Q$-functions, and the $t=-1$ specializations of Hall--Littlewood
functions for classical root systems.
\name{Ang{\`e}le M. Foley} and \name{Ronald C. King} prove determinantal and
Pfaffian identities for ninth-variation skew Schur functions and
$Q$-functions \cite{FoleyKing2021}.  Their approach uses non-intersecting
lattice paths and gives ninth-variation analogues of outside decomposition
identities.
\name{Wataru Takeda} and \name{Yoshinori Yamasaki} prove quadratic relations
for ninth variations of Schur functions and apply them to Schur multiple
zeta functions \cite{TakedaYamasaki2025x}.
\name{Mikhail Goltsblat} extends Macdonald's ninth variation to classical
group characters of types $A$--$D$ \cite{Goltsblat2023}.  The paper proves
Littlewood-type identities for these generalized characters and obtains
Jacobi--Trudi and N\"agelsbach--Kostka identities for factorial and rational
versions.
