\section[schurBottom]{Bottom Schur functions}

\begin{polydata}{schurBottom}

The bottom Schur functions were introduced by \name{Peter Clifford} and
\name{Richard Stanley} in \cite{CliffordStanley2004}.

Let the degree of the \hyperref[powerSum]{power-sum symmetric functions} be
$1$, so that $\deg(\powerSum_i)=1$ and
$\deg(\powerSum_\nu) = \length(\nu)$.  The
\hyperref[schurMurnaghanNakayama]{Murnaghan--Nakayama rule} states that
\[
\schurS_\lambda = \sum_{\mu} \frac{\chi^\lambda(\mu)}{z_\mu} \powerSum_\mu
\]
The \defin{bottom Schur function}\label{bottomSchurFunction}
$\hat{\schurS}_\lambda$ is defined as
\[
\hat{\schurS}_\lambda
=
\sum_{\mu : \length(\mu)=\text{lowest}}
\frac{\chi^\lambda(\mu)}{z_\mu} \powerSum_\mu,
\]
where the sum is over partitions contributing to the lowest-degree part in
the power-sum expansion.

The bottom Schur functions can be expressed as the lowest-degree term in a
\hyperref[schurJacobiTrudi]{Jacobi--Trudi} type determinant of power-sum
symmetric functions.
\name{Peter Clifford} and \name{Richard P. Stanley} prove this by deleting
from the Jacobi--Trudi matrix every row and column containing a $1$, and then
replacing each $\completeH_i$ by $\powerSum_i/i$
\cite[Sec.~3]{CliffordStanley2004}.
They also prove that the span of the bottom Schur functions of degree $n$ has
a basis indexed by partitions $\lambda \vdash n$ with
$\length(\lambda)=\mathrm{rank}(\lambda)$ \cite[Thm.~4.3]{CliffordStanley2004}.
Consequently its dimension is the number of partitions of $n$ into parts
differing by at least $2$, equivalently by the Rogers--Ramanujan identity,
into parts congruent to $\pm1$ modulo $5$.
For a related variant of $\hat{\schurS}_\lambda$, they show that the same
coefficients occur in the power-sum expansion and in the augmented monomial
symmetric-function expansion \cite[Thm.~7.4]{CliffordStanley2004}.


\begin{conjecture}[Alexandersson, 2019]

Let $R=(R_{\lambda\mu})$ with $\lambda,\mu \vdash n$ be the matrix with
coefficients defined by
$\powerSum_{\lambda} = \sum_\mu R_{\lambda\mu} \monomial_\mu$.


In \cite{CliffordStanley2004}, the authors consider $R-D$, where $D$ is the
diagonal of $R$, and compute the dimension of the nullspace of $R-D$ as $n$
increases.  The initial values are
\[
1,1,1,2,2,3,4,5,7,9,11,15,19,24,\dotsc
\]
They did not guess any combinatorial interpretation of these coefficients.

In 2019, an OEIS search suggested that the sequence $a(n)$ given by
\oeis{A129528} matches.
The sequence $a(n)$ is defined as follows:
Consider all Dyck paths, consisting of steps $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$.
The \defin{peak-abscissa-sum}\label{peakAbscissaSum} of a path is the sum of
the $x$-coordinates of all peaks.
Then $a(n)$ counts Dyck paths of \emph{any length} with
peak-abscissa-sum $n$.

\end{conjecture}

\name{Sylvie Corteel} and \name[J. S. Kim]{Jang Soo Kim} introduce lecture hall
Schur functions in \cite{CorteelKim2018}.  They prove analogues of the
Jacobi--Trudi identity and its dual.
Lecture hall tableaux generalize lecture hall partitions and anti-lecture
hall compositions, and contain reverse \hyperref[prelimTableaux]{semistandard
Young tableaux} as a limiting case.  Their generating functions appear as the
Schur-expansion
coefficients of multivariate little $q$-Jacobi polynomials, and the moments
of these polynomials give product formulas for lecture hall tableaux of fixed
\hyperref[prelimPartitions]{Young diagram} shape.
\name{Sylvie Corteel} and \name[J. S. Kim]{Jang Soo Kim} enumerate bounded
lecture hall tableaux \cite{CorteelKim2019x}.  Their formulas relate these
tableaux to \hyperref[prelimTableaux]{standard and semistandard Young
tableaux}, and identify the count with a coefficient in a
\hyperref[schurS]{Schur expansion} of
$\schurS_\lambda(m+y_1,\dotsc,m+y_n)$.

A \defin{lecture hall tableau}\label{lectureHallTableau} of type $(n,\geq,>)$
and shape $\lambda/\mu$
is a filling $T$ of the cells of $\lambda/\mu$ with nonnegative integers such
that
\[
  \frac{T(i,j)}{n+j-i}
  \geq
  \frac{T(i,j+1)}{n+j+1-i}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \frac{T(i,j)}{n+j-i}
  >
  \frac{T(i+1,j)}{n+j-i-1}
\]
whenever the cells involved are present.

\begin{example}
For $n=3$ and $\lambda=(2,1)$, the filling
\begin{figure}
\begin{ytableau}
2 & 1\\
1
\end{ytableau}
\end{figure}
is a lecture hall tableau of type $(3,\geq,>)$.  Indeed,
$2/3\geq 1/4$ along the first row, and $2/3>1/2$ in the first column.
\end{example}

For asymptotics of lecture hall tableaux, see \cite{CorteelKeatingNicoletti2019x}.


\section[schurWreath]{Wreath product Schur polynomials}

Wreath product Schur polynomials appear in the study of $G \wr \symS_n$;
see, for example, \cite{Macdonald1980} and \cite{MendesRemmelWagner2004}.

For the combinatorics, see \cite{IngramJingStitzinger2009}.
These polynomials are indexed by $r$-tuples of partitions,
$\underline{\lambda}$, and live in the space $\spaceSym^{\otimes r}$.
Define the \defin{wreath product Schur polynomials}
\label{wreathProductSchurPolynomial} as
\[
\schurS_{\underline{\lambda}}(\xvec^{(1)},\dotsc,\xvec^{(r)})
=
\prod_{i} \schurS_{\lambda^i}(\xvec^{(i)}).
\]
They can be realized as a sum over $r$-tuples of semistandard Young tableaux
of shape $\lambda^i$, where tableau $i$ only uses entries from the alphabet
$\xvec^{(i)}$.


A \hyperref[schurMurnaghanNakayama]{Murnaghan--Nakayama rule} is described
in \cite{MendesRemmelWagner2004}.
\name{Rijubrata Kundu} and \name{Papi Ray} give a combinatorial relation
between certain irreducible character values of $G\wr\symS_n$ and
$\symS_{rn}$ when $G$ is abelian of order $r$ \cite{KunduRay2025x}.  This
extends earlier character-value comparisons for the hyperoctahedral group and
wreath products.
\name{James Janopaul-Naylor} and \name[C. Ryan Vinroot]{C.~Ryan Vinroot}
study multipartition Kostka numbers $K_{[\lambda(j)]\mu}$, defined by
sequences of semistandard Young tableaux of multipartition shape and total
weight $\mu$ \cite{JanopaulNaylorVinroot2015x}.  These numbers occur as
multiplicities in permutation representations of wreath products, and the
paper characterizes when such a multiplicity is one.
