\section[oeisProofA189912]{A proof for A189912}

This page is part of the \hyperref[oeisProofs]{OEIS proof collection}.

The sequence \oeis{A189912} is defined as
\[
a_n \coloneqq \sum_{k=0}^n
\frac{n!}{(n-k)! (\lfloor k/2 \rfloor!)^2 (\lfloor k/2 \rfloor +1)}.
\]
Splitting the sum into even and odd indices gives
\begin{align*}
 a_n
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
   \frac{n!}{(n-2j)!(j!)^2(j+1)} \\
 &\quad+
   \sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}
   \frac{n!}{(n-2j-1)!(j!)^2(j+1)}.
\end{align*}
Rewrite the odd summand using
$1/(n-2j-1)!=(n-2j)/(n-2j)!$.  When $n$ is even, extending that
sum to $j=n/2$ adds a zero term.  Hence
\[
 a_n=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
 (n+1-2j)\frac{n!}{j!(j+1)!(n-2j)!}.
\]
The factor
$n!/(j!(j+1)!(n-2j)!)$ is the triangular array \oeis{A055151}.
This proves \name{Werner Schulte}'s conjecture from October 23, 2016.
