\metatitle{Real-rooted polynomials: Catalan families}

\metadescription{Real-rootedness and interlacing for Narayana polynomials,
Motzkin polynomials, Dyck path statistics, and related Catalan families.}
\metakeywords{real-rooted polynomials,Narayana polynomials,Motzkin
polynomials,Dyck paths,Sturm sequences}


\section[realRootedCatalan]{Catalan families}

Catalan combinatorics is a rich source of real-rooted families:
\hyperref[qtCatalan]{Dyck paths}, ballot sequences, Motzkin paths, and
related objects.  Many examples below are \hyperref[sturmSequence]{Sturm
sequences}, so consecutive polynomials interlace and every polynomial in the
sequence is real-rooted.
Other examples use \hyperref[hadamardProduct]{Hadamard products},
\hyperref[stablePolynomial]{stable-polynomial} refinements, or
\hyperref[polyaFrequency]{Pólya frequency and total-positivity} methods.
For the broader context, see
\hyperref[polynomialRootIntro]{unimodality and real-rootedness}.

\subsection[narayanaPolynomialFamiliesRealRooted]{Narayana polynomial families}

\subsubsection[classicalNarayanaPolynomialsRealRooted]{Classical Narayana polynomials}

\begin{example*}[Narayana polynomials]\label{ex:narayanaSturm}

Let $P_n(t)\coloneqq \sum_{\pi \in Dyck(n)} t^{peaks(\pi)}$,
where $Dyck(n)$ is the set of Dyck paths of size $n$, and $peaks(\pi)$
counts the number of peaks.  Equivalently, this counts descents in binary
words with $n$ zeros and $n$ ones such that, in each prefix, there are at
least as many zeros as ones; see \oeis{A001263}.
A closed-form formula is
\[
P_n(t) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^n \binom{n}{k} \binom{n}{k-1} t^k.
\]
These satisfy the recursion (see \cite[Eq.~(2)]{Sulanke2002} and
\cite[Eq.~(3.3)]{LiuWang2007})
\[
(n+1) P_n(t) = (2n-1)(1+t)P_{n-1}(t) - (n-2)(1-t)^2 P_{n-2}(t),
\]
with initial conditions $P_1(t)=t$, $P_2(t)=t(1+t)$, which is used to prove
the interlacing property.

Real-rootedness and the interlacing property were first proved in
\cite{Branden2006}.
See also \cite[Eq.~(31)]{Agapito2014}, where a different recursive formula,
involving derivatives, is listed:
\[
 (n+1)(n+2) P_{n+1}
 = t\bigl((1-t)^2 P''_{n} + 2(1+2n)(1-t)P'_{n}
 + (1+2n)(2+2n)P_n\bigr).
\]
Another approach is given in \cite{KostovFinkelshteinShapiro2009}.

\begin{lstlisting}

NN[1] := t;
NN[2] := t (1 + t);
NN[n_] := NN[n] = Expand[
	(
		(2 n - 1) (1 + t) NN[n - 1] -
		(n - 2) (1 - t)^2 NN[n - 2]
)/(n + 1)];
\end{lstlisting}

The recurrence above gives
a \hyperref[sturmSequence]{Sturm-sequence} proof; the differential recurrence
can be handled by interlacing criteria for derivative recurrences; stable
multivariate refinements give a \hyperref[stablePolynomial]{stability}
proof; and the \hyperref[narayanaTransformations]{Narayana transformation}
explains why the type $A$ and type $B$ Narayana bases preserve real roots on
nonnegative coefficient cones.

One can also prove real-rootedness by observing that the Narayana polynomials
are a simple transformation of Jacobi polynomials, or Gegenbauer polynomials
(which are orthogonal polynomials on an interval).
The Narayana polynomials are given by the hypergeometric series
\[
P_n(t) = {}_2F_1(-n, -n-1;2;t)
\]
or, in Wolfram-language notation,
\texttt{HypergeometricPFQ[{-n, -n - 1}, {2}, t]}, see
\cite[Sec.~12]{BarryHennessy2011}.


Yet another recurrence is
\[
(1 + n)P_n=(3nt + n - t - 1) P_{n-1} - 2 t(t-1) P'_{n-1}
\]
which can be proved using the first theorem in \cite{Dung2022x}.

\name{Shi-Mei Ma}, \name{Jean Yeh}, and \name{Yeong-Nan Yeh} give a
context-free grammar for the Narayana polynomials \cite{MaYehYeh2023x}.
Their differential-operator framework also gives standard-Young-tableau
interpretations for several Eulerian and Narayana-type polynomial families.

\name{Harold R. L. Yang} and \name{Philip B. Zhang} introduce stable
multivariate refinements of the type $A$ and type $B$ Narayana polynomials
\cite{YangZhang2024x}.  Their polynomials are generating functions for
labeled plane trees with weights recording improper edges, and real stability
is proved using the Borcea--Brändén characterization of stability-preserving
linear operators.
\name{Frédéric Chapoton} and \name{Guo-Niu Han} study the roots of the
Poupard and Kreweras polynomials \cite{ChapotonHan2020}.
\end{example*}


\begin{example*}[Type $B$ Narayana polynomials]
The type $B$ Narayana polynomials can be defined as
\[
P_n(t) = \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}^2 t^k.
\]
Their coefficients form \oeis{A008459}.
Since these are produced by the Hadamard product of $(1+t)^n$ with itself, it
follows that the $P_n(t)$ are also real-rooted.
See the \hyperref[hadamardProduct]{Hadamard product subsection} for a general statement.
They also occur as the type $B$ endpoint of the
\hyperref[narayanaTransformations]{Narayana transformation} discussed on the
main real-rootedness page.
\end{example*}

\name{Jiang Zeng} constructs a $q$-deformed continuous interpolation
between the type $B$ and shifted type $A$ Narayana polynomials.  For
$0<q<1$ and $\theta>-1$, its row polynomials have simple negative zeros and
are gamma-positive after palindromic normalization
\cite[Thm.~9.2 and Cor.~9.4]{Zeng2026qNarayana}.



\subsubsection[generalizedNarayanaPolynomialsRealRooted]{Generalized Narayana polynomials}

\begin{example*}[Generalized Narayana polynomials]
A two-parameter family of Narayana-type polynomials
\[
P_{n,m}(t) = \sum_{k=0}^n
\left(\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{k}
-\binom{n}{k+1}\binom{m}{k-1} \right)t^k
\]
is real-rooted for all nonnegative $m,n$.  If $n\geq m+2$, it has exactly
one positive and $m$ negative zeros, and the negative zeros of $P_{n,m}$
and $P_{n+1,m+1}$ interlace \cite[Thm.~3.4]{ChenYangZhang2018}.
The specialization $n=m+1$ is the Narayana triangle \oeis{A001263}.
Bounds and monotonicity for the unique positive zero are given in
\cite[Thms.~3.1 and~4.1]{Zhao2021x}.
\end{example*}


\begin{example*}[Boolean--Narayana numbers]

A family similar to the Narayana numbers, named the
\defin{Boolean--Narayana numbers}\label{booleanNarayanaNumberDefinition}, is
introduced by \name{Miklós Bóna} in \cite{Bona2026x}.
The corresponding polynomials are proved to be real-rooted.  The
numbers are
\[
 BoNa(n,k) =
 \frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{k-1}
 \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}2^j\binom{k-1}{j}\binom{n-k+1}{j+1}.
\]
If we define $P_n(x) \coloneqq \sum_{k} BoNa(n,k)x^k$, then these satisfy the
recursion
\[
 (n+1) P_n(x) = (2n-1)(1+x) P_{n-1}(x) - (n-2)(1-6x+x^2) P_{n-2}(x).
\]
The first polynomials are $P_1=x$, $P_2=x+x^2$, $P_3=x+4x^2+x^3$,
$P_4=x+9x^2+9x^3+x^4$ and $P_5=x+16x^2+38x^3+16x^4+x^5$, and the values
$P_n(1)=1,2,6,20,72,272,\dotsc$ are \oeis{A071356}.
\end{example*}


\subsection[dyckPathStatisticPolynomialsRealRooted]{Dyck-path statistic polynomials}

\begin{example*}[NNE instances in Dyck paths with fixed number of peaks]

In \cite{WangZhang2024}, the authors consider the polynomials
\[
 W_{n,k}(t) \coloneqq
 [x^k] \sum_{\pi \in Dyck(n)}
 x^{\mathrm{peaks}(\pi)} t^{\mathrm{nne}(\pi)},
\]
where $\mathrm{nne}(\pi)$ counts the number of North-North-East instances in
the path.
The authors show that $\{ W_{n,k}(t) \}_{n \geq k}$ for fixed $k$ form a
\hyperref[sturmSequence]{Sturm sequence}, and that
$\{ W_{n,k}(t) \}_{1\leq k \leq n}$ is \hyperref[sturmSequence]{Sturm-unimodal}.

The generalization to \hyperref[fussCatalanNNERealRooted]{Fuss--Catalan paths}
is real-rooted as well, and has the same zeros.


The following formula appears in
\cite{BonaDimitrovLabelleLiPappeVindasMelendezZhuang2025}.

Let $w_{n,k,m}$ be the number of Dyck paths of size $n$, with $k$
instances of $NE$ and $m$ instances of $NNE$.  Then the polynomials defined via
\[
W_{n,k}(t) =
\sum_{m\geq 0} w_{n,k,m}t^m =
\frac{1}{k} \binom{n}{k-1}
\sum_{m=1}^{\min(k,n-k)}
  \binom{n-k-1}{m-1}\binom{k}{m} t^m
\]
are real-rooted.  The formula above is valid for $n \gt k$, and for
$n \leq k$ the polynomials are constant.
The authors conjectured that for fixed $k$, $\{W_{n,k}\}_{n\geq k}$ form a
\hyperref[sturmSequence]{Sturm sequence}, and that for fixed $n$,
$\{W_{n,k}\}_{1 \leq k\leq n}$ is
\hyperref[sturmSequence]{Sturm-unimodal}.
These conjectures were later proved in \cite{WangZhang2024}.
Summing over $k$ gives the distribution of $NNE$ on Dyck paths,
the triangle \oeis{A091156}.
They find a two-term recurrence involving \(W_{n-1,k}(t)\).  Alternatively,
\(W_{n,k}(t)\) can be expressed in terms of \(W_{n,k-1}(t)\) and its
derivative.
\end{example*}


\begin{example*}[NNE instances in Fuss--Catalan paths]
\label{fussCatalanNNERealRooted}
For $m\geq1$, let $\mathcal{D}^{(m)}_n$ be the lattice paths with $n$ north
steps and $mn$ east steps that stay weakly above the line from $(0,0)$ to
$(mn,n)$, and put
\[
  W^{(m)}_{n,k}(t)\coloneqq [x^k]\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{D}^{(m)}_n}
  x^{\#NE(\pi)}t^{\#NNE(\pi)}.
\]
For $m=1$ these are the polynomials $W_{n,k}(t)$ above.  For $m=2$ and $n=5$,
the polynomials for $k=1,\dotsc,5$ are $t$, $10t+10t^2$, $45t+45t^2$, $120t$
and $42$.  Real-rootedness was conjectured by Alexandersson in 2023.

\begin{theorem}[\name{Ryan Halper}, 2026, \cite{Halper2026FussCatalan}]
For $m\geq1$ and $1\leq k\leq n$,
\[
  W^{(m)}_{n,k}(t)=\frac{1}{k}\binom{mn}{k-1}C_{n,k}(t)
  =\binom{mn}{k-1}\binom{n}{k-1}^{-1}W_{n,k}(t),
  \qquad
  C_{n,k}(t)\coloneqq\sum_{j\geq1}\binom{k}{j}\binom{n-k-1}{j-1}t^j,
\]
with $C_{n,n}(t)=1$.  In particular, every $W^{(m)}_{n,k}(t)$ is real-rooted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Append one east step and write the path as
$N^{a_1}EN^{a_2}E\dotsm N^{a_L}E$ with $L=mn+1$ and $a_1+\dotsb+a_L=n$.
By the cycle lemma, exactly one of the $L$ cyclic rotations of any vector
$(a_1,\dotsc,a_L)$ of nonnegative integers with sum $n$ comes from a path in
$\mathcal{D}^{(m)}_n$, and the $L$ rotations are distinct.  Peaks correspond to
the positive entries $a_i$ and $NNE$-factors to the entries $a_i\geq2$, and both
are preserved by rotation.  Choosing the $k$ positive positions and a
composition of $n$ into $k$ parts gives
$W^{(m)}_{n,k}(t)=\frac{1}{L}\binom{L}{k}C_{n,k}(t)$, which is the stated
formula.  For $m=1$ it is the formula of
\cite{BonaDimitrovLabelleLiPappeVindasMelendezZhuang2025} above, so
$W^{(m)}_{n,k}(t)$ is a positive multiple of $W_{n,k}(t)$ and has the same
zeros.
\end{proof}

Halper also gives a direct argument for the zeros of $C_{n,k}(t)$.  With
$r=n-k>0$ and $f(x)=x^k(1+x)^{r-1}$,
\[
  \frac{f^{(r)}(x)}{r!}=\frac{x^k}{1+x}\,C_{n,k}\!\left(\frac{1+x}{x}\right),
\]
and by Rolle's theorem all zeros of $f^{(r)}$ lie in $[-1,0]$.

Since only a positive factor depends on $m$, the interlacing results of
\cite{WangZhang2024} hold for every $m$: for fixed $k$, the sequence
$\{W^{(m)}_{n,k}(t)\}_{n\geq k}$ is a \hyperref[sturmSequence]{Sturm sequence},
and for fixed $n$, $\{W^{(m)}_{n,k}(t)\}_{1\leq k\leq n}$ is Sturm-unimodal.
\end{example*}


\begin{example*}[Peaks on unit interval graphs]
\label{unitIntervalPeaksRealRooted}
Through \hyperref[areaSequence]{area sequences}, Dyck paths of semilength
$n$ correspond to \hyperref[unitIntervalGraph]{unit interval graphs} on
$[n]$, and peaks become maximal cliques, the \defin{peak-cliques}.  The
\defin{peak-nesting} of the graph is the largest number of peak-cliques
containing a common vertex.  Over all unit interval graphs, peaks give the
\hyperref[narayanaPolynomialFamiliesRealRooted]{Narayana polynomials}.
\name{Per Alexandersson} and \name{Leonardo Saud Maia Leite} study
subclasses \cite{AlexanderssonLeite2026Peaks}.
\begin{itemize}
\item A graph is \defin{Abelian} if $[n]$ splits into two consecutive
 cliques; there are $2^{n-1}$ such graphs.  The peak-nesting and peak
 polynomials are
 \[
  \sum_{i\geq1}\binom{n}{2i-1}t^i
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \sum_{i\geq0}\binom{n}{2i}t^{i+1},
 \]
 and both families are real-rooted generalized
 \hyperref[sturmSequence]{Sturm sequences}
 \cite[Thms.~4.3 and~4.9, Cors.~4.5 and~4.10]{AlexanderssonLeite2026Peaks}.
\item A graph is \defin{$2$-nested} if its peak-nesting is at most two;
 these are the unit interval graphs that are line graphs of triangle-free
 multigraphs.  The peak polynomial
 \[
  \frac{t}{2}\Bigl((1+t+\sqrt t)^{n-1}+(1+t-\sqrt t)^{n-1}\Bigr)
 \]
 forms a Sturm sequence, and the peak-nesting polynomials and connected
 versions are real-rooted generalized Sturm sequences
 \cite[Thm.~5.6, Cors.~5.5, 5.7 and~5.9]{AlexanderssonLeite2026Peaks}.
\item A graph is \defin{reduced} if no two adjacent vertices are twins.
 Its peak polynomial is $t^n M_{n-1}(1/t)$, where $M_{n-1}$ is the
 \hyperref[motzkinPolynomialsRealRooted]{Motzkin polynomial} below; the
 connected version is real-rooted as well
 \cite[Thm.~6.10, Cor.~6.14]{AlexanderssonLeite2026Peaks}.
\end{itemize}
Over all unit interval graphs, the peak-nesting polynomials are not
real-rooted.
\end{example*}


\begin{example*}[Peaks on Catalan words]
\label{catalanWordPeaksRealRooted}
A \defin{Catalan word}\label{catalanWordDefinition} of length $n$ is a word
$w$ with $w_1=0$ and $0\leq w_{i+1}\leq w_i+1$.  These are the area
sequences of Dyck paths, that is, the increasing
parking functions shifted down by one.
A \defin{peak} of $w=w_1\dotsm w_n$ is an index $1\lt i\lt n$ with
$w_{i-1}\lt w_i\gt w_{i+1}$, and $\operatorname{pk}(w)$ is the number of peaks.

\begin{theorem}[ProgramComputer, 2026, {\cite[Thm.~1]{ProgramComputer2026CatalanPeaks}}]
\label{conjCatalanWordPeaks}
The peak polynomials of Catalan words of length $n$,
\[
  C_n(t)\coloneqq\sum_{w}t^{\operatorname{pk}(w)}
  =\sum_{i\geq0}\frac{1}{n-i}\binom{n-i}{i}\binom{2n-3i}{n-2i-1}(t-1)^i,
\]
have only simple negative zeros, and $C_n(t)$ and $C_{n+1}(t)$ strictly
interlace for every $n$.
\end{theorem}

Real-rootedness and consecutive interlacing were conjectured by
Alexandersson in 2026.  The proof makes an
\href{https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/59}{earlier
public argument} for all sufficiently large $n$ effective, with the explicit
bound $n\geq150$, and settles the cases $n\lt150$ by direct computation.
No proof by a recurrence or a combinatorial interlacing argument is known.
Both statements also have a
\href{https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/312293aa850010e9d9509d6850a482b9c7b7966f/topics/catalan-word-peaks/lean/CatalanStrictPeaksAllInterlacing.lean}{Lean
formalization}.
% Lean: CatalanStrictPeaks.strict_peaks_all_lengths; axioms propext,
% Classical.choice, Quot.sound in the repository's CI audit (not locally built).

The coefficients form \oeis{A116424}, which also counts Dyck paths of
semilength $n$ by occurrences of $UDUU$.  The generating function
$G=1+\sum_{n\geq1}C_n(t)x^n$ satisfies
\[
  G=1+x^2(1-t)G+x(1-x+tx)G^2,
\]
and \name{Jean-Luc Baril}, \name{Pamela E. Harris},
\name{Kimberly J. Harry}, \name{Matt McClinton}, and
\name{José L. Ramírez} give it in closed form
\cite[Thm.~18]{BarilHarrisHarryMcClintonRamirez2025}.
\[
\begin{array}{rl}
\toprule
n & C_n(t) \\
\midrule
3 & 4+t \\
4 & 9+5t \\
5 & 22+19t+t^2 \\
6 & 57+66t+9t^2 \\
7 & 154+221t+53t^2+t^3 \\
8 & 429+729t+258t^2+14t^3 \\
9 & 1223+2391t+1131t^2+116t^3+t^4 \\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
By contrast, descents on Catalan words give
$\sum_k\binom{n-1}{2k}2^{n-1-2k}\mathrm{Cat}_kt^k$, a rescaled
gamma-polynomial of the
\hyperref[classicalNarayanaPolynomialsRealRooted]{Narayana polynomial},
which is therefore real-rooted.
\end{example*}


\subsection[motzkinPolynomialsRealRooted]{Motzkin polynomials}

\begin{example*}[Motzkin polynomials]

The noncrossing matching polynomial of the complete
graph $K_n$ is the Motzkin polynomial, see \oeis{A055151}.
These polynomials satisfy the recursion
\[
 (n+2)M_n(x)
 = (2n+1)M_{n-1}(x) - (n-1)(1-4x)M_{n-2}(x),
 \qquad M_1(x)=1,\quad M_2(x)=x+1.
\]
Using the methods in \cite[Sec.~3]{Wagner1992},
one can use induction to show that the roots of $M_{n-1}(x)$ interlace those
of $M_{n}(x)$.  In particular, these polynomials are real-rooted.
